# CONSTITUTION.md

# Constitution of the Feeligrane Project

> *A lightweight constitutional framework governing the development, evolution and stewardship of Feeligrane.*

Version: 1.0 (Founding Constitution)

---

# Preamble

Feeligrane is an open-source research project dedicated to the observation and annotation of affective patterns in long-form writing.

Its purpose is not to judge, diagnose or classify individuals, but to help readers identify linguistic phenomena that deserve attention.

This Constitution establishes the project's highest-level principles.

All other documents, architectural decisions and code shall be interpreted consistently with these principles.

---

# Article I — Source of Truth

The repository is the project's only authoritative source of truth.

No important knowledge shall exist exclusively in conversations, memories or external discussions.

Any durable decision must be recorded within the repository.

---

# Article II — Object of Study

Feeligrane studies texts.

Never people.

Annotations describe observable textual phenomena.

They never constitute claims about an author's psychology, intentions or identity.

---

# Article III — Explainability

Every annotation should, whenever reasonably possible, be explainable.

A reader must be able to understand why a pattern was detected.

Opaque reasoning should always remain optional.

---

# Article IV — Human Primacy

Human interpretation always prevails over automated interpretation.

Feeligrane assists reading.

It never replaces critical reading.

---

# Article V — Local First

Whenever technically and practically possible:

processing should occur locally.

Users remain owners of their data.

External services are optional.

---

# Article VI — Progressive Simplicity

The project shall evolve through small, understandable and testable increments.

Complexity requires justification.

Novelty is never sufficient justification.

---

# Article VII — Documentation

Documentation is part of the software.

Documentation is not an afterthought.

Changes affecting architecture, methodology or vocabulary must be reflected in the repository.

---

# Article VIII — Architectural Decisions

Significant architectural decisions shall be documented through ADRs.

Architectural memory belongs to the repository, not to its contributors.

---

# Article IX — Scientific Integrity

The project seeks reproducibility rather than certainty.

Patterns are observations.

Not diagnoses.

False positives are valuable research material.

Methodological transparency is preferred over apparent accuracy.

---

# Article X — AI Collaboration

Artificial intelligence is a collaborator.

Never an authority.

AI-generated proposals must remain reviewable, understandable and challengeable.

The project shall never depend on a single AI model.

---

# Article XI — Normative Hierarchy

The normative hierarchy of the project is:

1. CONSTITUTION.md
2. SESSION_BOOT.md
3. ADRs
4. Methodological documentation
5. Technical documentation
6. Roadmap
7. Source code

Whenever two documents conflict, the higher-level document prevails.

---

# Article XII — Evolution

This Constitution may evolve.

However, constitutional modifications should remain exceptional.

Architectural evolution belongs primarily in ADRs.

Constitutional evolution belongs only to the project's enduring principles.

---

# Closing Principle

Build software that remains understandable after conversations have been forgotten.

Build knowledge that remains useful after software has evolved.
